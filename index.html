Mission Text: The Robotics Runoff

Read once for what happens. Read again looking for the clues you will need in the missions.

The Robotics Runoff

Maya and Devin had spent three weeks building a small robot for the school technology showcase. Maya wanted to run one final safety test before their presentation. Devin glanced at the clock and shook his head. “We don’t have time. I fixed the turning code last night. Trust me.”

Maya folded her arms. “Last time we skipped a test, the robot drove straight into a chair.” Devin sighed, but instead of waiting, he placed the robot on the floor and started the program.

The robot rolled forward, spun too sharply, and clipped the edge of their display sign. The sign crashed onto the table. “Great,” Maya snapped. “Now we have a broken sign and a robot that still turns wrong.” Devin’s face reddened. “I was trying to save us time.”

Ms. Chen, the showcase coordinator, walked over. She told them their presentation would begin in twenty minutes. When she left, Devin stared at the crooked sign. “You were right. I rushed it.” Maya took a breath. “Then help me fix it. You adjust the code. I’ll repair the sign.”

Devin slowed the turning speed while Maya taped the sign from behind. Before their turn, they tested the robot twice. This time it followed the path perfectly. When the judges arrived, Maya introduced the design and Devin demonstrated the robot. Their project did not win first place, but the judges praised how clearly the pair explained the problem they had solved together.

1

🔗 Mission 1: Spot the Interaction

Teach It: An interaction happens when characters respond to one another through words, actions, reactions, or decisions. A character trait by itself is not an interaction.

Model It: Devin says they should skip the test; Maya warns him not to. That exchange is an interaction because each character responds to the other.
Strong-reader thinking: Ask: What does Character A do or say? How does Character B respond?

➡️ Mission 2: Build the Cause-and-Effect Chain

Teach It: For this benchmark, identifying the interaction is only the beginning. You must connect it to what happens next in the plot.

Model It: Devin ignores Maya's warning → he starts the robot → it hits the sign → the pair now has a new problem to solve.
Strong-reader thinking: Use the frame: Because ___ interacted by ___, the next plot event was ___.

⚡ Mission 3: Interaction & Conflict

Teach It: Character interactions often create, increase, or reduce conflict. Track how one response changes the tension.

Model It: After the sign falls, Maya snaps at Devin and Devin defends himself. Their disagreement increases the conflict before they begin solving it.
Strong-reader thinking: Ask whether the interaction makes the problem worse, keeps it going, or begins to solve it.

🔄 Mission 4: Find the Turning Point

Teach It: A turning point is a moment when the direction of the plot changes. Character interactions often cause that shift.

Model It: Devin admits Maya was right. Maya responds by inviting him to work with her instead of continuing the argument.
Strong-reader thinking: The plot turns because the characters change how they interact.

🧩 Mission 5: Connect Interaction to Resolution

Teach It: At the end of a story, look backward. Which earlier interaction made the resolution possible?

Model It: Their choice to cooperate leads to the successful retest and presentation.
Strong-reader thinking: A strong answer links the interaction to several later events, not just the very next sentence.
Practice 9 DOK 2

Practice 10 DOK 3

6

🕵️ Mission 6: Prove the Plot Connection

Teach It: A complete analysis names the interaction, explains the plot effect, and supports the explanation with a specific detail.

Model It: Weak: “They work together.” Strong: “After Devin admits he rushed, Maya asks him to help repair the project. Their cooperation leads to two successful tests and allows them to present.”
Strong-reader thinking: Interaction + resulting event(s) + evidence = benchmark-level analysis.
